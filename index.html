What is Genetic Engineering?

Genetic engineering is the process of changing an organism's DNA using modern biotechnology. Scientists can insert, remove or alter genes to give an organism new characteristics or improve existing ones.

This technology has transformed medicine by helping produce insulin, vaccines and potential gene therapies. It has also improved agriculture through crops that are more resistant to pests, drought and disease.

Applications

Genetic engineering is used in many fields including medicine, farming, environmental science and biological research. Scientists continue developing safer and more precise methods such as CRISPR gene editing.

Benefits

  • Treating inherited diseases
  • Producing important medicines
  • Improving food production
  • Increasing resistance to disease in crops

Ethical Questions

Although genetic engineering offers many benefits, it also raises important ethical questions about safety, fairness, environmental impacts and how the technology should be regulated.
